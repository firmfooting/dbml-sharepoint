# Research ethics register — staff guide

Three lists that answer one question between them: **what have we sent to the
ethics committee, and may it actually run here?**

You will use one of two roles. If you are running a project, read *For
investigators*. If you keep the register, read *For the research governance
officer* as well.

## The one thing to understand first

**Ethics approval and site authorisation are two different decisions.**

| | Who decides | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Ethics approval** | The external Human Research Ethics Committee | The project is ethically acceptable |
| **Site authorisation** | This service's Chief Executive or delegate, on the research governance officer's assessment | The project may be conducted *here*, with these resources, by these people |

An approved project that has not been authorised **cannot start at this
service**. That is not a formality and it is not the governance office being
slow: authorisation is where insurance, indemnity, resourcing and local
suitability are checked, and it is given against evidence that the committee
has approved the project.

The register shows both, always side by side, plus a **Site Readiness**
column that reads the two and tells you the answer:

| Site Readiness | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Ready to start here** | Go |
| **Ethics cleared - site authorisation outstanding** | Wait. Chase the governance office, not the committee |
| **Site authorised - ethics not cleared** | Stop. Something is wrong, or the approval has expired |
| **Neither gate cleared** | Nothing has been decided yet |
| Not approved / Withdrawn / Suspended at this site | The project is not running here |

A project whose **Project Stage** says *Underway* while its site
authorisation is not *Authorised* has its whole row washed red in *Current
projects*. If your project is one of those rows, ring the research governance
officer today.

## For investigators

### Lodging a project

Create a row in **Projects** as soon as you decide to submit — not after the
committee replies. An empty register is not the same as an empty pipeline,
and the governance office needs to see what is coming.

The New form asks for six things and none of them is hard:

- **Title**, worded as the ethics application words it. The two documents
  have to be findable from each other.
- **Project Type** — research, quality improvement, clinical audit,
  evaluation or a student project. All of them belong here.
- **Department**, **Principal Investigator** (name them as the application
  does, even if they are at another organisation) and **Site Investigator**
  (you, or whoever answers for this project *here*).
- **Summary**, in plain language. **No participant information of any kind.**

Then the **Review pathway** section: which pathway the project takes, and
what it does with people. If you do not know the pathway yet, leave it at
*Not yet determined* — that is an honest answer and the governance office
would rather have it than a guess.

### Lodge the SSA at the same time, not afterwards

The commonest avoidable delay in this whole process is submitting the
site-specific assessment only once ethics approval arrives. The two
assessments run on different things and can run at the same time; the
authorisation still cannot be *given* until the approval exists, but all the
work before it can be done in parallel.

Fill in **SSA Submitted Date** on the day you lodge it. The gap between that
date and **Authorisation Date** is the only measure anybody has of how long
local governance takes.

### While it is under review

- **Ethics Status** *Response required* means the committee has asked for
  something and **the ball is with you**. It is the only status that means
  that. Every other one means you are waiting.
- **Review Rounds** counts how many times they have come back. Add one each
  time. Past about five, the register's governance says the application needs
  a conversation rather than another written response, and the bar being full
  is what says so.

### Once it is approved

Three things people miss, in the order they bite:

1. **Conditions are not the approval.** If the letter says "approved subject
   to ...", set **Conditions Status** to *Outstanding* and paste the
   committee's wording verbatim into **Approval Conditions**. Paraphrase is
   how a condition quietly stops being met. Move it to *Met* only when it
   actually is — *Conditions outstanding* is a view somebody works.
2. **Write down the expiry.** **Ethics Approval Expiry** is the date the
   approval runs out. Nothing computes it and nothing renews it for you; the
   *Approvals expiring* view is what stops it surprising you.
3. **Write down the next report date.** **Next Report Due** is when the
   committee next expects to hear from you.

### Amendments

Anything that changes what was approved — the protocol, the participant
materials, the investigators, the sites, the approval period — is an
amendment, and it goes to the committee **before** you do it.

Create a row in **Amendments**, and the field that matters most is **Site
Impact**:

| Site Impact | What has to happen here after the committee approves |
|---|---|
| *No local action needed* | Nothing. Implement it |
| *Notify research governance* | Tell the governance office. The Site Cleared Date is the day you did |
| *Requires site re-authorisation* | The site has to authorise the amended project again |

**An amendment the committee has approved is not usable here until the local
action is done.** Leave **Site Cleared Date** blank until it is; that blank
is what puts the row into *Approved, not cleared locally*, which is where the
governance officer will find it.

### Reports

Create a **Reports** row for every report you owe, with its **Due Date**, as
soon as you know it. **Annual progress reporting is a condition of continued
approval** — miss it and the approval can lapse, which is a far bigger
problem than writing the report was.

Three other report types exist and are the ones people forget: an **interim**
report the committee asked for, a **safety or adverse event** report, and a
**protocol deviation or breach** report. The last two usually go to your
**local research governance officer as well as to the committee** — check
your service's procedure, and record both in the Notes.

A report is not finished when you send it. It is finished when
**Acknowledged Date** exists. Until then the row stays in *Outstanding
reports*, which is correct.

## For the research governance officer

Your daily list is **Current projects**, and your worklist is two views:

- **Approved, not authorised** — projects the committee has cleared and this
  service has not. Oldest decision first, because the longer a project has
  been sitting there the likelier somebody has assumed otherwise. Work this
  to empty.
- **Conditions outstanding** — approvals somebody will quote as
  unconditional.

Two sweeps, monthly, both described in `50-govern/governance.md`:

- **Approvals expiring** — anything inside 90 days. Extension amendments take
  time; a project that reaches its expiry has to stop.
- **Outstanding reports** and **Due in 60 days** — everything owed. The red
  rows at the top of *Outstanding reports* are already late.

**The register does not know today's date.** Site Readiness is calculated
from the two gate columns only, so an approval that expired last week still
reads *Ready to start here* until somebody moves Ethics Status to *Expired*.
Moving it is your job, and it is why the expiry sweep is a named duty rather
than a nice idea.

## What never goes in these lists

No participant names, no identifiers, no clinical detail, and **no
recruitment or screening numbers, not even totals**. This register holds
project metadata: who, what, which committee, what was decided, when it
expires, what is owed. The report itself — which does contain participant
information — lives in your records system, and **Report URL** links it.

If you are about to type a number of people into a box here, that is the
moment to stop and read `50-govern/governance.md`.
