# Volunteer register — coordinators' guide

*Coordinators only — the register is not visible to general staff, and
volunteers interact with you, not with the list.*

## Onboarding a volunteer

The form has four sections and they run in the order the work happens.

1. **VL_Volunteer** → **New** at first contact. *Who they are*: name,
   intended role, team, an emergency contact. *Coordination*: yourself as
   Coordinator. *In the programme*: Status **Applying** — it is already
   the default, and while it stands the **Start Date** field is not on the
   form at all.
2. *Checks and clearances*, per the role matrix (governance): sight the
   police check and/or WWCC, record **expiry dates and the WWCC number** —
   the register holds dates and numbers; the sighted documents are handled
   per your privacy procedure (most programmes record-and-return rather
   than copy).
3. Induction done → **Induction Date**. The form refuses a date in the
   future, with a message saying so.
4. Everything required in place → Status **Active**. The **Start Date**
   field appears the moment you do, and the form will not save an Active
   volunteer without both a start date and an induction date. **Not
   before** — Applying volunteers don't start, however lovely they are;
   the matrix is the gate, and the form holds the two parts of it that a
   rule can hold.

## The monthly sweep (15 minutes, non-negotiable)

1. *Checks expiring 90 days*: contact each volunteer about renewal now —
   checks take weeks, and an expired check means standing them down. An
   expiry already past renders red with a warning icon wherever it
   appears, so the top of this list is the urgent end.
2. Anything **expired**: the volunteer pauses (Status **Inactive**, a
   kind phone call, a note) until the renewal is sighted. This is the
   uncomfortable conversation the register exists to make rare — 90 days
   of warning means it almost never happens. Inactive volunteers keep
   being swept: their dates stay coloured, because they intend to return.
3. *Missing checks*: every active volunteer missing a police check, a WWCC
   *or* an induction date. It is deliberately wider than the matrix — the
   view cannot know which roles need which check, so it shows all three
   gaps and you apply the matrix. A row here that turns out not to need a
   WWCC is ten seconds of reading; a row nobody ever saw is a governance
   failure.

## Keeping it human

The register is compliance; the programme is people. Notes carry
availability, skills and role history — never health details, family
circumstances or performance commentary. Write every note as if the
volunteer will read it; under privacy law, they may.

## Breaks and exits

- Extended break → **Inactive** (checks keep being swept if they intend
  to return).
- Leaving → **Exited**; see governance for what gets trimmed and when.

Both land in the *Inactive and exited* view, which is where the annual
retention trim is worked from. An Exited volunteer's expiry dates stop
being coloured — they are history at that point, not a deadline.
