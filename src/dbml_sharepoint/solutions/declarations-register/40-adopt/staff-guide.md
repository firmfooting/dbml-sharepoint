# Declarations register — staff guide

*The rule of thumb for both registers: if you're wondering whether to
declare, declare. Nobody was ever criticised for a declaration; careers end
over the absence of one.*

## Declaring an interest (2 minutes)

Declare when your private interests **could appear** to intersect with your
duties — appearance is the test, not your own confidence in your integrity.
New role on a board? Partner's business tenders to us? Relative in your
reporting line? Declare it.

1. **DR_Interest** → **New**.
2. **Title**: the interest in one neutral line.
3. **Type**, **Declared date** (today), and **Detail**: the facts of the
   interest and where it could touch your role. Don't self-assess — that's
   the coordinator's job with your manager. The form makes that literal:
   **Status**, **Management plan**, **Review date** and **Ceased date**
   are not on the New form at all, so there is nothing there to
   self-assess with.
4. Save. You can't edit it afterwards; if the interest changes materially,
   submit a fresh declaration and tell the coordinator the old one has
   evolved.

**When the interest ends** (you leave the board, the company is sold), tell
the coordinator — they'll record the cessation.

## Declaring a gift, benefit or hospitality (1 minute)

Offered anything by someone who deals with the organisation — a bottle, a
dinner, tickets, travel? **Declare it whether you accepted or not** (yes,
declined offers too — patterns of offers are exactly what the register
exists to see).

1. **DR_GiftBenefit** → **New**: what, who offered it, when, estimated
   value, context. **Decision** and **Decided by** are not on the New
   form — you don't decide your own gift, and the form doesn't let you
   pretend otherwise.
2. Above the threshold in the governance rules? Don't accept until the
   decision comes back. Every new row starts at **Pending decision**,
   which shows amber in every view, and clearing those is a standing
   monthly job for the coordinators.

## The views

### DR_Interest

| View | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Live interests** | The default. Everything not ceased, grouped by who declared it. |
| **My interests** | Just yours. This is the annual attestation. |
| **Awaiting assessment** | Declarations nobody has assessed yet, oldest first — the coordinators' ten-day clock. |
| **Reviews due** | Managed conflicts due for review inside the next thirty days, with the management plan. |
| **Ceased** | Interests that have ended, most recently ceased first. |

### DR_GiftBenefit

| View | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Last 12 months** | The default. A *rolling* twelve months, most recent first. |
| **Pending decisions** | Offers still awaiting a decision, oldest first. No date window — an old pending decision is worse, not less relevant. |
| **Annual disclosure** | The same rolling twelve months, highest value first. This is the report. |
| **By offeror** | Everything, grouped by who offered it. Repeat offerors are exactly what this register exists to see. |

None of these is something you build, and none should be renamed — a
redeploy puts the declared name back.

## The annual attestation

Once a year you'll be asked to confirm your declarations are current. Open
**My interests**: it shows your rows and nobody else's, and it shows a
different set to each person who opens it. Two-minute read, not a
form-filling project. That's the payoff of declaring as you go.
