# Starting a project

## Start here, and stop here

`programme-governance` is the whole of a project's first week on one site: the
risks, actions, issues and decisions read together in the project meeting
rather than in four places, and the activities behind them carrying exactly one
Responsible and one Accountable each.

It is ten lists, which is more than a new project needs. That is fine. Deploy
it, use the four delivery lists, and leave the rest empty. An empty list costs
nothing, appears in no view you have to read, and is already there on the day
you find you need it. The alternative, standing up a second family later,
means a second site or a second prefix and a decision about which one holds
what.

**Do not add anything else on day one.** The two below solve problems a new
project does not have yet, and adding them early is how a project ends up
maintaining registers instead of running.

## Add later, when you feel the absence

**`meeting-actions`**, when the meeting itself becomes the record. It carries a
`Meeting` list, which `programme-governance` has no equivalent of: a date, who
attended, and the minutes, with the decisions and actions hanging off it. Add
it when somebody asks what was agreed at a meeting three weeks ago and the
answer is not in anybody's notes.

**`stakeholder-contacts`**, when the people outside the project outgrow one
list. `GOV_Stakeholder` is one flat register of who the project points at. This
one is three levels: an organisation, its named contacts, and a dated log of
every interaction with them. Add it when you are tracking a relationship rather
than a name.

A risk that outgrows the project graduates to `risk-register`, on the four
criteria in `programme-governance`'s governance notes.
