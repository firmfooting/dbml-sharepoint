# Service requests — staff guide

## Requesting (everyone — 2 minutes, once)

Need something fixed, moved, supplied or set up?

1. **SR_Request** → **New**.
2. **Title**: what and where — "Meeting room 2 screen not detecting
   laptops". **Category** picks whose queue it lands in. **Location**
   precisely (the fixer shouldn't need to phone you to find it).
3. **Priority**: *Urgent* means blocking work or safety — not "before my
   meeting if possible". Mis-marked urgencies slow real ones.
4. **Detail**: what's needed and why; attach a photo if it helps.
5. Save — then **stop**. No follow-up email, no corridor reminder, no
   second request. Track it in *My requests*. That discipline is the deal
   that makes the queues work: you get visibility, teams get one channel.

If it's genuinely urgent-urgent (safety, outage): phone the team, then
submit so it's on record.

## Working the queue (service teams)

- **Your queue, twice a day.** Urgent first, then oldest. The queue is the
  commitment — inbox requests get redirected to the list, kindly, every
  time ("pop it in the list so it doesn't get lost between us").
- **Pick up**: set **AssignedTo** (yourself), Status **Accepted** — the
  requester sees someone owns it. That single status change eliminates most
  chasing.
- **Blocked on parts or a decision**: Status **Waiting - parts or
  approval**, with a word in Resolution — visible waiting beats silent
  waiting.
- **Done**: Status **Completed**, **CompletedDate**, one line in
  **Resolution**. Days-to-complete computes itself.
- **Declining** is allowed and honest — Status **Declined** with the why
  ("needs budget approval — raise via change request"). A clear no beats a
  slow nothing.
