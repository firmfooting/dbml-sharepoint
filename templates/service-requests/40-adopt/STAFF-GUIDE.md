# Service requests — staff guide

## Requesting (everyone — 2 minutes, once)

Need something fixed, moved, supplied or set up?

1. **SR_Request** → **New**.
2. **Describe the request**: **Title** — what and where ("Meeting room 2
   screen not detecting laptops"). **Category** picks whose queue it lands
   in. **Location** precisely (the fixer shouldn't need to phone you to
   find it). **Detail**: what's needed and why; attach a photo if it helps.
3. **Triage**: **Priority**. *Urgent* means blocking work or safety — not
   "before my meeting if possible". Mis-marked urgencies slow real ones.
4. Save — then **stop**. No follow-up email, no corridor reminder, no
   second request. Track it in **My requests**, which shows every request
   you raised, newest first. That discipline is the deal that makes the
   queues work: you get visibility, teams get one channel.

The form doesn't ask you who will do the work or when it was finished —
those fields aren't on the New form because they aren't yours to fill in.

If it's genuinely urgent-urgent (safety, outage): phone the team, then
submit so it's on record.

## Working the queue (service teams)

**Your queue, twice a day.** The list opens on **Open by category** —
every live request, grouped by category and collapsed, urgent first and
then oldest within each priority. **Facilities queue** and **IT queue**
are the same thing filtered to one team. The queue is the commitment;
inbox requests get redirected to the list, kindly, every time ("pop it in
the list so it doesn't get lost between us").

- **Pick up**: set **Assigned To** (yourself), Status **Accepted** — the
  requester sees someone owns it. That single status change eliminates
  most chasing.
- **Blocked on parts or a decision**: Status **Waiting - parts or
  approval**. **Resolution** appears as soon as you do — say what you're
  waiting for and when you'll chase it. That line shows in the **Waiting**
  view, which is what the weekly review reads; visible waiting beats
  silent waiting.
- **Done**: Status **Completed**, then **Completed Date** and
  **Resolution** appear. The list will not let you save without both.
  Days-to-complete computes itself and draws a bar in the **Turnaround**
  view, coloured by the request's priority — so eleven days on an urgent
  job is red and eleven days on a low-priority one is not.
- **Declining** is allowed and honest — Status **Declined** with the why
  ("needs budget approval — raise via change request"). A clear no beats a
  slow nothing, and the same save rule applies: a decline needs a date and
  a reason.
